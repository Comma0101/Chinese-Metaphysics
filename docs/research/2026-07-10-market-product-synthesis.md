## Research Quality and Limits

The research corpus contains 47 competitor records, 73 evidence claims, and 53 market-model rows. It combines official demographics, primary competitor pages, platform listings, public customer language, academic work, and scenario models.

Its strengths are breadth, transparent uncertainty, current competitor pricing, and explicit counterevidence. Its limitations are decisive:

- the research agent did not inspect the actual repository;
- there is no representative dataset for premium BaZi purchase behavior among overseas Chinese professionals;
- social and marketplace language is directional, not prevalence evidence;
- behavioral rates in the market-sizing model are hypotheses;
- legal, payment, and platform findings require live revalidation and specialist review;
- no Zhiji customer, conversion, retention, delivery-time, refund, or CAC data exists yet.

## Market Definition

Zhiji sits at the overlap of four categories:

1. professional BaZi/Mingli consultation;
2. culturally specific uncertainty and identity work;
3. premium human advisory services;
4. software-assisted case delivery and longitudinal review.

Direct alternatives include named BaZi practitioners, multi-practitioner firms, AI report products, chart tools, consultation marketplaces, and gig-market reports. Adjacent alternatives include Western astrology, Human Design, tarot/psychic marketplaces, life or executive coaching, therapy, and general AI. Substitutes include family, friends, religious advisers, domain professionals, free calculators, and delay.

Customers already search for BaZi readings, 八字咨询, a master, reports, and a second perspective. This supports retaining BaZi in the category label while modernizing the value and ethics.

## Demand Evidence

The evidence supports these conclusions:

- Large Chinese diaspora populations exist in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia, New Zealand, Singapore, and Malaysia.
- A meaningful professional and high-income tail exists, especially in North American metropolitan clusters.
- People transact across a wide category ladder, from automated reports and marketplaces to named practitioners charging hundreds or more.
- Public buyers complain about conflicting charts, generic AI prose, remedy upsells, opaque billing, missing records, and ratings based on immediate emotion rather than delayed outcomes.
- Buyers value another perspective, blind spots, a reference framework, human clarification, a durable report, and no forced object sale.

The counterevidence matters more than the broad population counts:

- General U.S. survey evidence suggests much spiritual-service use is casual or entertainment-led.
- Heavy reliance for major decisions is rare in the general population.
- Younger and lower-income consumers may participate more often, which cuts against a broad affluent-professional thesis.
- Educated skeptics require more proof and may prefer credentialed coaching or professional advice.
- Free calculators and general AI make long reports, visual polish, and generic personalization commodities.

The research therefore validates “people pay in this category,” not “this ICP will pay Zhiji US$388–488.”

## Initial ICP

**Primary:** A culturally close Chinese professional, approximately 28–45, in North America, with one decision due within 90 days and prior openness to BaZi or adjacent interpretive systems.

**Secondary:** Founders and executives reached through warm introductions after the founder has delayed outcome proof and a documented method.

**Later:** Consenting couples/families, Singapore professionals, and other English-speaking diaspora markets.

**Not initial:** Students with low ability to pay and elevated vulnerability, mainland residents, minors, or people seeking health/legal/financial predictions or repeated reassurance.

### White Space

No sampled competitor visibly combines all of:

1. documented birth-time, place, time-zone, and method normalization;
2. a versioned deterministic calculation trace;
3. historical calibration that records misses and ambiguity;
4. one concrete decision and deadline;
5. traceable substantive human review with case and reviewer-role provenance;
6. task-specific AI disclosure;
7. a no-remedy/no-affiliate firewall;
8. a client-controlled durable record;
9. a 30/90-day outcome review.

Each feature is copyable. The potential defensibility comes from consistent execution, consented longitudinal outcomes, practitioner QA, and reputation.

## Product Model

### Model A — Premium Concierge

Use this to learn the real work during the first 20–30 cases. It offers high trust and low capital needs but limited capacity.

### Model B — Productized Human Plus Software

This is the intended full-product direction. Software handles deterministic calculation, consent, scheduling, payment, evidence organization, secure records, report workflow, and review measurement. Zhiji remains operationally accountable through substantive human review, stable internal reviewer-role IDs, method versions, and correction history without publishing a personal identity.

### Model C — Consumer Platform

Do not pursue until at least 100 paid human-led cases establish demand, stable schemas, safe boundaries, practitioner QA, and healthy lower-accountability willingness to pay.

## AI Boundaries

AI may assist with intake structure, note organization, retrieval, translation, formatting, consistency checks, and follow-up preparation. It must not calculate the canonical chart probabilistically, issue unreviewed consequential interpretations, infer sensitive facts not supplied, imitate a human master, conceal its role, encourage dependency, or provide health/legal/financial predictions.

Disclosure should be literal: software assists with specified tasks; Zhiji completes and records substantive human review before delivering an interpretation.

## Pricing and Unit Economics

Current category observations span free/low-cost reports, marketplace services around tens of dollars, established human consultations around the low hundreds, and premium named practitioners at US$588–1,600+.

The recommended starting ladder is a US$49 credited/refundable calibration deposit, a US$388 founding core, and a US$488 target core. These remain hypotheses. Record actual practitioner minutes, admin time, software cost, refunds, payment fees, acquisition labor, and cohort source for every case.

## Go-to-Market

Prioritize trust transfer over scale:

1. paying-client referrals;
2. professional, alumni, founder, and diaspora communities;
3. exact-intent search;
4. XiaoHongShu message testing;
5. WeChat/email nurture;
6. long-form practitioner content and small events.

Do not treat views, followers, saves, community size, or general spirituality interest as qualified demand.

## Open Questions

- Will qualified customers trust Zhiji's method, case provenance, and human-review controls without a public practitioner identity?
- Where will the entity and practitioner legally operate?
- Will a processor approve the actual service in writing?
- What calculation method will be canonical?
- Can calibration be fixed before personal events are disclosed?
- Which label produces qualified paid intake rather than curiosity?
- Will buyers accept disclosed AI assistance?
- Can delivery remain under 3.5 hours without hollowing out the human value?
- Can one non-friend channel keep CAC below 25% of first-order revenue?
- Do delayed reviews improve clarity and agency without driving dependence?
